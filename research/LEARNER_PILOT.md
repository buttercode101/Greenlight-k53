# GreenLight learner evidence pilot

Status: protocol and local data capture prepared. No participants recruited, no results collected, no outcome claim justified.

Question: Does the integrated learning and targeted-practice path improve performance on unseen K53-style questions and delayed recall?

Recruit 20–40 consenting learners across supported codes and a realistic range of lower-cost Android devices. Record the code and device/browser, not names, identity numbers or licence documents. Participants may use the app without joining research. Obtain consent separately before receiving any exported history; the app does not transmit it automatically.

Assess at baseline, after 1–2 weeks and after a further retention interval. Use independently reviewed parallel assessments on the same concepts, distinct from the practice bank and each other. Balance forms to avoid an easier post-test. If feasible, allocate comparison groups randomly and record other study materials used. Do not infer causality from a small self-selected before/after group.

Measure each section's accuracy and completion, unseen question performance, delayed recall, drop-off points and usability problems. Examine code/device subgroups descriptively and report sample sizes and missing follow-ups. Session elapsed time includes idle/background time and must not be labelled active study time.

The app's progress page exports first-attempt correctness, separate-day recall, per-question counts and up to 100 completed local session outcomes. Existing history has no invented first-attempt baseline. Exported files contain question-level results; participants choose whether to share them. Avoid copying identifying filenames into public results. Define retention/deletion arrangements before accepting data.

Actual DLTC results are a separate, optional self-reported outcome with explicit consent. Never publish a pass-rate improvement or superiority claim from software tests, testimonials, practice completion, or a small uncontrolled sample.

Before publishing: independent assessment review, documented sample/analysis, privacy review, transparent limitations and a readable account of results, including negative or inconclusive findings.
